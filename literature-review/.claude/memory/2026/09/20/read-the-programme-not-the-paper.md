# A programme's output has to be read against itself

A set of papers from one group, each citing the others, is not N independent
results. The per-paper summaries can all be accurate and the set still not add
up. Three checks earn their keep, and none of them is visible from a single
paper.

## 1. Do the numbers reconcile across papers?

One paper measures a quantity on a real specimen; a sibling paper's model
implies the same quantity. Compare them. In one case the measured value and the
value implied by the other paper's stated geometry differed **by a factor of
two**, neither paper cited the other's figure, and neither reconciled them. That
gap is a finding in itself — it usually means the modelled object is not the
built object.

## 2. Were the specimens made the way the model assumes?

The telling pattern: one paper states plainly that prototypes were
**machined** rather than formed by the intended production process, because
suppliers had minimum order quantities — while a sibling paper's process diagram
shows the production route as the plan. The experimental evidence base then
rests on specimens whose geometry may not match what was simulated, and the
sibling papers reuse those specimens without repeating the caveat. **One paper's
disclosed limitation silently becomes the whole programme's undisclosed one.**

## 3. Is each paper's headline metric the one the next paper needs?

A concept paper optimises for one figure of merit; the follow-on measurement
papers never measure that quantity at all. The headline claim then rests
entirely on a single model with no validation anywhere in the set. Worth stating
explicitly in a synthesis: *which* claim is unvalidated, and *which* paper would
have had to validate it.

## Method note

A parallel deep-read works best when every reader gets an **explicit output
template** — problem, method, every number with its condition, figures, stated
vs unstated limitations, what is reusable, open questions — and is told to write
"not given" rather than fill a gap. The template is what makes the reports
comparable enough to cross-check; without it the inconsistencies above stay
buried in prose.
